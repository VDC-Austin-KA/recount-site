The problem with cloud dictation on site

Most dictation tools send your audio to a server. That is fine at a desk and useless in a basement, a crawlspace, an attic, or a rural property twenty minutes off the highway. You speak, nothing happens, and you end up typing the findings twice — once into your phone's notes, again into the report that evening.

Recount has no server to reach. The speech model is inside the program, running on your processor. Turn off your wifi and it behaves identically.

It already knows the vocabulary

Two people, one recording

Walking a client through the property while recording? Turn on speaker labelling and Recount separates the voices — and recognises you again when you start talking after the client has finished, rather than just noting that somebody changed. 

Speaker 1: The north elevation shows efflorescence along the foundation.
Speaker 2: Is that something we need to worry about?
Speaker 1: It indicates moisture moving through the masonry.

It clusters voices, it does not know names — whoever speaks first is Speaker 1. People talking over each other get assigned to one of them, not both.

Being straight about the limits

It is a young product and there are things it does not do:

  • Windows only. No Mac, no iOS, no Android. If you write reports on a tablet in the field, this is not for you yet.
  • It types into whatever window has focus. It does not integrate with Spectora, HomeGauge or any other report platform — it fills in the box your cursor is already in.
  • The installer is not code-signed yet, so Windows shows a "protected your PC" warning on first run. Click More info, then Run anyway. A certificate is being obtained.
  • English only.
